What this is, and what it isn't

A public service campaign, not another app to depend on.

Unplugged isn't meant to be the AI app you use every day for years. You're welcome to chat with it as often as you want, for as long as you want. It's yours and it's free. But that's not why it exists.

It exists so that everyday people, especially people who aren't technical, can see for themselves what AI really is, and don't get swept up in the hype of corporate AI. Once you've seen it, you'll know you have options.

It's one file.

The AI inside Unplugged is a single 720 MB file: Google's free Gemma model. No server, no cloud, no company on the other end.

It's not magic.

Every word is calculated by your own processor or graphics card. The app shows you the graph spike while it writes.

It can't read you back.

The AI file is read-only. It doesn't record what you type, doesn't learn from you, and doesn't know who you are. The app proves it with a fingerprint check.

Heads up: your computer will warn you. We know.

When you open Unplugged the first time, Windows or macOS will show a scary-looking warning. That's because the app isn't "code-signed", which costs hundreds of dollars every year. Unplugged is free and has nothing to sell, so we skipped it. The warning means "we don't know who made this", not "this is dangerous". The code is public on GitHub for anyone to check.

Why this matters

Let's be honest: the AI in this app is small. It isn't as smart as the big cloud AIs today, and that's okay. That's not the point.

Corporations are not the enemy.

Many of them build amazing things. One of them, Google, even gave away the AI inside this app for free.

But they'll want you to believe that the only way to have the AI of the future is to pay them for it, every month, forever.

That is simply not true.

And local AI will keep getting better, year after year, as long as it's allowed to stay open and free.

Some people will tell you that local AI is dangerous.

Like anything else, it can be used for good or for bad.

But they'll try to tell you it's too dangerous for you to have freedom and complete privacy. Privacy that hasn't existed since the dawn of the internet age.

Don't let them get away with it.

What about data centers?

People have real concerns about data centers: the electricity and water they use, and what they mean for the towns around them. Those concerns deserve to be taken seriously.

But local AI doesn't replace data centers. The AI in Unplugged was trained in one. Today there's simply no other way to train large, capable models, and that work keeps a lot of people employed, including software engineers like me.

So this isn't local AI versus data centers. Data centers train the models. Local AI lets you run them on your own terms.

My own guess: as models keep getting more efficient, we'll need fewer data centers in the future, not more. We're just not there yet.

If AI only exists on company servers, every thought you share with it is stored on someone else's computer. Local AI is how privacy survives the AI age.

Not all "local AI" is the same.

Some companies will tell you their AI is "local" because it runs on your machine. But running on your machine doesn't automatically make it private, or yours.

Some of those same apps won't hesitate to collect data about how you use them, or to decide which questions you're allowed to ask. And it will all be done in the name of security and protection.

Security and protection are important. But they can also be used to manipulate you, for commercial interests.

How to tell if your AI is really local:

  • Turn off your internet. Does it still work, completely? Real local AI doesn't need to phone home.
  • Read what it collects. Look for "usage data", "analytics", "telemetry" or "diagnostics" in its privacy policy or settings. Real local AI has nothing to collect.
  • Can you choose the AI model? If you can swap in any open model you like, you're in control. If you can't, someone else is.
  • Can anyone check the code? Open-source apps can be inspected by anyone. "Trust us" isn't proof.

Unplugged passes all four: it works with the internet off, collects nothing, uses a standard open model file you can replace with another one, and every line of its code is public.

Questions

Is Unplugged meant to be my everyday AI app?

Not really, though you can use it as much as you like, for as long as you like. Unplugged is a public service campaign: a free, five-minute proof that AI can live on your own computer. If you want to go further, the open community has wonderful everyday apps. See where to go next.

Is it as smart as ChatGPT?

No, not today, and that's not the point. This small AI proves AI can live on your own computer with no internet at all. Local models are improving fast: in a few years the same and better will run on ordinary computers, as long as open models stay available.

Do I need the internet to use it?

Only once, to download it, like buying a book. After that, never again.

Does it work without a graphics card?

Yes. It runs on your computer's regular processor. A graphics card makes it faster, but it isn't required.

What happens to what I type?

Nothing. It stays in your computer's temporary memory and disappears when you close the app. It's never sent anywhere and never saved. The AI file itself is read-only and can't record anything.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

There's no catch. No account, no ads, no data collection, no upgrade. Keep AI Local is one software engineer with nothing to sell, who wants people to know this is possible.

Which AI is inside?

Google's Gemma 3 1B, the smallest Gemma made for conversation. Gemma is provided under and subject to the Gemma Terms of Use found at ai.google.dev/gemma/terms, including the Gemma Prohibited Use Policy.

Why does my computer warn me about Unplugged?

Because we didn't pay for code signing. Microsoft and Apple charge yearly fees to "vouch" for an app; without that, they show a warning for any download they don't recognize. Unplugged is a free project with nothing to sell, so we skipped it. You can read every line of the code on GitHub, and check each download's fingerprint on its release page. See how to open it.

Does this website track me?

No. No cookies, no analytics, no trackers, no fonts or scripts loaded from anywhere else. It would be a strange way to talk about privacy otherwise.

Who's behind this?

Just me: a software engineer. There's no company behind this, no investors, no ads, and nothing to sell. Unplugged is free, and I have no stake in it at all.

I simply believe that everyone, everywhere, should have options when it comes to AI.

The first time I realized that the true power of AI lay not on a cloud server, but on my desktop, inside my own computer, it changed my life.

I hope it changes yours too.
